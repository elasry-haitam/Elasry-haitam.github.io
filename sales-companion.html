01. The Problem and Operational Friction

At Allianz Partners (operating in 75+ countries with 22,600+ employees), commercial sales teams routinely handle multi-million euro Requests for Proposals (RFPs) and complex partner tenders. Historically, assembling a single RFP dossier consumed 8 to 12 hours of manual labor per document.

Account managers had to manually comb through hundreds of fragmented presentations, legal policy wordings, and regional SLA guidelines scattered across SharePoint. Beyond pure operational latency, this created three structural bottlenecks:

  • Knowledge Drift and Stale Claims: Sales teams often relied on outdated local copies of pitch decks, pitching retired coverage models, superseded SLA commitments, or inconsistent commercial pricing structures.
  • Contractual Liability and Hallucination Risk: In B2B insurance and assistance contracts, submitting an unverified commitment in an RFP response legally binds the enterprise. Standard conversational LLMs tend to generate fluent but speculative promises when confronted with ambiguous edge cases.
  • The Enterprise Adoption Ceiling: Prior centralized AI initiatives reached fewer than 3% active adoption across the sales community. The friction was operational: mandatory single-sign-on web portals disconnected from local files, rigid IT installation barriers, and network latencies when uploading large confidential tenders.

The Objective: Deliver an autonomous, local-first system capable of answering technical queries, drafting complete RFP responses, composing branded executive presentations, and tracking market intelligence, while operating strictly within verified internal sources and requiring zero external server infrastructure.

02. Hard Enterprise Constraints

Most academic and open-source agent frameworks assume a frictionless greenfield environment: managed cloud containers, remote vector databases, and unbounded external API access. In an enterprise operating under strict data governance, this paradigm fails immediately.

03. High-Level Architecture and Intent Routing

Rather than building a chaotic swarm of conversational sub-agents communicating through unconstrained message passing (which introduces high latency, compounding token costs, and non-deterministic state loops), Sales Companion utilizes a Hierarchical Orchestrator-Skill Pattern:

Figure 1: Hierarchical Orchestrator and Inline Skill Architecture

Why Markdown Skills over Framework Agents?

Skills are structured instruction files written in declarative Markdown inside skills/ (covering QA, RFP Q&A, Narrative Proposals, Meeting Prep, Presentations, Research, Competitive Intelligence, and Solutioning). They are loaded directly into the orchestrator's context upon intent detection without spawning separate operating system processes.

This design eliminates inter-process latency and serialization overhead. Furthermore, because skills are human-readable Markdown, commercial stakeholders and legal compliance officers can review and audit the AI's decision boundaries directly in Git without deciphering Python code.

Sub-Agent Boundary: When a task requires genuine isolation or heavy non-blocking compute (such as extracting structured metadata across hundreds of new documents during library indexing), the orchestrator dispatches a dedicated child agent (index-subagent) running as a distinct background subprocess with its own localized memory space.

04. Engineering Deep Dives and Trade-Offs

Deep Dive A: Tiered Retrieval : Why Flat JSONL Beat Vector DBs

Conventional generative AI literature recommends ingesting all corpus documents into a vector database (such as Pinecone, Qdrant, or Chroma) and querying via cosine similarity on dense embeddings. In our enterprise testing, pure vector retrieval exhibited critical failure modes:

  • Semantic Collisions: Dense embeddings frequently struggled to distinguish between subtle territorial differences. Two assistance policies from Germany and Switzerland often share 98% lexical similarity while differing on a single crucial geographic exclusion clause.
  • Operational Overhead: Running an external vector database container violated the zero-dependency enterprise workstation constraint.

The Engineered Solution: A Two-Tier Retrieval Architecture

  • Tier 1 (Instant Metadata Filter): All ~314 verified internal documents are indexed into a local, compact document_library.jsonl file. Each entry contains structured metadata: primary intent, keywords, line of business, target market, client industry, and high-level summaries. The orchestrator queries this manifest in a single deterministic pass.
  • Tier 2 (Normalized Full-Text Retrieval): When deep technical substance is required, the engine navigates directly to normalized, clean Markdown files via explicit md_path pointers. Raw source documents (.pptx, .docx, .pdf) are normalized offline once, guaranteeing deterministic text extraction.
  • Strict Scope Discipline: Enforced hard rules prohibiting the LLM from merging geographic scopes. Facts from distinct sources retain their individual boundary tags. Every output concludes with an auditable block: Source file, Search Depth (JSONL vs. MD read), and Confidence Level (High, Partial, Low).

Deep Dive B: Two-Pass Document Processing for 50+ Question RFPs

Enterprise tenders frequently arrive as dense questionnaires spanning 50 to 150+ granular questions across technical capabilities, IT security, SLAs, and commercial governance. Feeding the entire tender into a single prompt causes severe context dilution, hallucination, and session timeouts.

To solve this, we architected a Two-Pass Pipeline:

  • Pass 1 (Research and Retrieval Mapping): The pipeline parses the document (.docx, .xlsx, or .pdf), decomposes the questionnaire into individual structured questions, and searches the indexed knowledge library. It produces a comprehensive Research Map pairing every question with its verified internal citations. No narrative answers are drafted at this stage.
  • Pass 2 (Bounded Section Generation and Assembly): A dedicated Python engine (rfp_section_writer.py) iterates through the research map section-by-section. It generates precise, sourced responses within bounded context windows, injecting them directly into the official corporate Word template using python-docx while preserving brand styles, typography, and table layouts.
  • Strategic Proactive Suggestions Layer: Following answer generation, the engine executes a differential analysis against company differentiators. It surfaces up to 5 strategic value-adds that the prospect did not explicitly ask for but that strengthen the proposal. Each suggestion is grounded in internal case studies and highlighted in green for executive review.

This two-pass design completely eliminated token timeouts and reduced end-to-end tender draft turnaround from 8 to 12 hours to 13 to 23 minutes.

Deep Dive C: Declarative Presentation Engine and The 3-Judge Quality Loop

The earliest prototype attempted to have the LLM write arbitrary Python scripts using python-pptx to draw shapes and position text. This approach suffered from frequent syntax errors, overlapping text boxes, and inconsistent brand alignments.

We overhauled the engine to enforce a Strict Declarative Contract:

  • Content Generation: The LLM is restricted to generating a strictly validated JSON schema (deck.json) defining the narrative story flow, slide layout archetypes (hero, split-screen, grid, timeline), and textual proof points.
  • Deterministic Rendering: A Python rendering engine interprets the declarative schema, calculating pixel-perfect element coordinates, typography scales, brand colors, and icon alignments.

To maintain quality across iterations, we implemented a 3-Judge Evaluation Harness:

  • Judge 1 (Deterministic Heuristic): A 100-point deterministic checker evaluating structural density, character thresholds, margin compliance, and accessibility standards.
  • Judge 2 (Multimodal Vision Judge): Automatically converts rendered slides to high-resolution PNG images, feeding them to a vision model to evaluate visual balance, whitespace distribution, and aesthetic clarity.
  • Judge 3 (Content Judge): An LLM judge evaluating narrative coherence, client positioning, and persuasive rigor.

Deep Dive D: Autonomous Competitive Intelligence (Haiku / Sonnet Model Cascade)

To provide sales teams with real-time market positioning, we architected an automated competitive intelligence crawler monitoring 6 major European assistance competitors across 66 data sources (press portals, product releases, financial filings, review hubs).

Scraping dozens of dynamic web pages generates immense noise (cookie updates, navigational changes, marketing fluff). Routing all raw HTML to heavy frontier models would have been prohibitively expensive and slow.

We engineered an automated Two-Tier Model Cascade:

  • Tier 1 (Claude 3 Haiku): High-throughput, zero-shot relevance triage and web page diffing. Discards 99% of boilerplate updates and flags only genuine strategic signals.
  • Tier 2 (Claude 3.5 Sonnet): Invoked exclusively on validated market signals to extract structured JSON entities: competitor entity, event category (M&A, OEM partnership, new product), confidence score, and strategic impact analysis.
  • Deduplication and Corroboration: Applied SHA-256 content hashing to eliminate identical articles across syndication feeds, paired with Jaccard similarity thresholding (0.45) for multi-source event corroboration.
  • Async Resilience: Integrated bounded coroutines (asyncio.wait_for) to isolate headless Playwright browser deadlocks, paired with explicit process tree termination to prevent zombie background processes on Windows.

The pipeline tracked 238 validated market events with a 15 to 25:1 call-to-event filtering ratio, operating completely autonomously for less than ~$30/month in total compute cost.

06. Key Engineering Lessons Learned

1. Declarative Schemas Beat Free Code Generation

Constraining generative models to produce strictly validated declarative data contracts (JSON or Markdown) rather than executable code eliminates 95% of runtime failures, prevents security escapes, and ensures deterministic layout execution.

2. Real-World Constraints Drive Superior Architecture

The mandate to run locally without server dependencies initially appeared restrictive. In practice, it led to a zero-maintenance, infinitely portable system that completely bypassed corporate IT procurement roadblocks and network latencies.

3. Automated Evaluation is an Essential Engineering Investment

Anyone can prompt an LLM; proving that a prompt adjustment or model update does not introduce subtle regressions requires golden benchmark datasets and multi-judge evaluation suites.
